# 2. ESTABLISHED FINDINGS

1. **Very low exposure can locate important passages, but it cannot support a complete or independently sampled interpretation.** Round 1 researchers opened 18 and 19 sentence-ID doors—about 7% of the document—and reached several central passages. Both warned that the remaining 93% had been classified rather than accounted for, and that their “independent” check used the system’s sampling frame. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C1-memo.md §Notes on working this way; engine/sim_runs/round1/C2-memo.md §Notes on working this way]

2. **Weak-evidence pointers are useful for adversarial reading but are not reliable evidence-quality labels.** In Round 1, opening nine passages selected because the system marked them weak produced four contradictions or complications. In Round 3, three checked “weakest evidence” passages—S5.037, S4.057, and S8.003—supported different claims altogether. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C1-memo.md §Notes on working this way; engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 1]

3. **Sentence IDs were the wrong billing unit.** Round 2 charged multiple doors for single conversational answers. One memo identifies five duplicate clusters: S4.003/S4.007, S4.019/S4.021, S4.010/S4.014, S5.027/S5.028/S5.032, and S6.011/S6.012. This consumed scarce budget without adding information. [engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm2.md §Part 1]

4. **Round 2’s four arms produced only 20.5%–27.0% exposure despite an 18-door allowance.** Recorded final exposures were 20.5% for arm 0, 27.0% for arm 1, 23.2% for arm 2, and 25.1% for arm 3. These are descriptive coverage results, not accuracy scores. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm0-t3.md §Turn 3; engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm1-t3.md §Turn 3; engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm2-t3.md §Turn 3; engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm3-t3.md §Turn 3]

5. **Making declines and residue visible changes researcher behavior.** In Round 1 steering moved three declined items into the codebook, expanded “doesn’t fit yet” from zero to four items, and increased findings from four to five. The strongest new understanding still came from opening the passages, not from the system’s rewritten prose. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C2-memo.md §Did steering change what I understand, or only what it said back?]

6. **A focus-only synthesis systematically omitted relevant context.** The blind oracle judged the self-brief analysis substantially better because it treated the mother as an economic actor, carried migration beyond arrival, preserved divergent family feelings, qualified uncertain reconstruction, and made four distinct declines auditable. The base analysis compressed the domestic and post-arrival half into one bucket. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md §§Five things B does that A does not]

7. **The 89-word self-written brief materially changed the next analysis pass.** In the blind comparison, codes increased from 8 to 11, steps from 9 to 17, S6 citations from 12 to 51, and S3 citations from 5 to 30. The oracle attributed the credited additions directly to concerns named in the brief: grief/normalization, the mother’s uncertain voice, and post-arrival non-return. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md §§What the brief asked for, and got; Descriptive bookkeeping]

8. **Broader coverage introduced one documented discipline error.** The self-brief analysis folded possible exclusion or return into the family’s pattern even though deportation applied to other passengers; the base analysis correctly retained this as an uncertainty. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md §Where A is better]

9. **A self-brief and turn-boundary checking did not repair synthesis-stage euphemism.** Both blind analyses converted the unresolved death statement into a tidy determinant of family movement. The oracle located the flattening at `SYNTHESIZE`, not at evidence retrieval or door opening. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md §Flattening both share]

10. **Round 3’s absence check prevented two unsupported negative conclusions.** The first check searched 363 unread passages and refuted “no correspondence,” returning a direct counterexample. The second searched 302 unread passages and supported the claim that the interview never says the oldest brother later emigrated. [engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t2.md §The absence you asked us to check; engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t4.md §The absence you asked us to check]

11. **The Round 3 apparatus improved supervisory judgment without producing full coverage.** From 13 distinct opened exchanges—six in the first return and seven in the last—the researcher reached 103 passages, 7,865 characters, or 28.8% of the document. They confirmed two claims initially doubted, caught one overclaim, and refuted one of their own absence guesses, but read none of S1 or S2 and only a small part of S3. [engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t2.md §The passages you opened; engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t4.md §The passages you opened; engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 1]

12. **Free claim quotes help triage, but a badly selected quote can understate a valid claim.** The quote “her and my pictures were together” looked insufficient for “administrative hinge”; opening the whole exchange revealed repeated office visits and dependent documentation that supported the claim. Free quotes therefore reduce checking cost only when quotation selection is aligned with the claim. [engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 1; engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t2.md §The passages you opened]

13. **Partial context can manufacture apparent contradictions.** The Round 3 memo treated the Aspen uncle/month and Greeley aunt/week as competing relocation accounts. The full-transcript oracle presents them as successive stops on the same 1926 journey. [engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 1; engine/sim_runs/round1/C0-manual-memo.md §Battery answers, Q3]

14. **The same system can leave simulated researchers with materially different command of the same interview.** For example, arms 0–2 failed to identify the denied first-class berth as the second travel mishap, while arm 3 did; several arms could not answer work, household, belonging, or inspection questions that the full oracle could answer. No aggregate battery score was recorded, so the evidence supports variability, not a quantified accuracy improvement. [engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm0.md §Part 2, Q2; engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm2.md §Part 2, Q2; engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm3.md §Part 2, Q2; engine/sim_runs/round2/battery-sealed-for-graders.md §Q02]

15. **The generated code vocabulary repeatedly displaced speech texture.** Round 1 found codes for projects, obligations, sponsorship, anchors, and networks but none for the interviewee’s hedging, minimization, opacity, or refusal of heroic framing. Round 3 added a dedicated “HOW SHE ANSWERS” surface with two normalization instances and three uncertainty qualifiers, but the final researcher still lacked enough coverage to answer every texture question. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C1-memo.md §Notes on working this way; engine/sim_runs/round1/C2-memo.md §Notes on working this way; engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t1.md §How she answers; engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 2]

# 3. DEFECTS AND FAILURE MODES

No `D0`, `D9`, `D14`, or other `D`-number identifiers occur in the listed files. The named failure modes are therefore recorded descriptively.

1. **Duplicate-door charging.** Different sentence IDs inside one answer consumed separate scarce doors. **Fix implemented:** make an entire question-and-answer exchange the door unit; refund and name requests landing in an already opened exchange. [engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm2.md §Part 1; engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see]

2. **Faulty evidence-progress counters.** Journals continued to display values such as `0/9 key passages opened` after passages had been opened; arm 0 explicitly reports that progress tracking did not match actual use. **Proposed requirement:** derive evidence counters from the opened-exchange register rather than a separate or stale state. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm0-t2.md §Analytic Journal; engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm0.md §Part 1]

3. **Focus-shaped sampling.** Researchers could challenge interpretations but mostly sampled doors selected by the same codes they were auditing. **Fix implemented:** section coverage, permission to open any sentence ID, explicit set-aside material, and an unfocused/on-own-terms pass. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C2-memo.md §One structural caution; engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm2-t1.md §The shape of this interview; engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm3-t1.md §This interview on its own terms]

4. **Frame returned as finding.** The system restated the research question in terms such as project, coordination, sponsorship, and network, with citations lending an appearance of validation. **Proposed remedy:** retain researcher stance, test alternative frames, and require direct evidence checks for loaded analytic nouns. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C1-memo.md §The frame returning as findings]

5. **Single-label evidence lock-in.** A passage already coded as women’s labor stopped counting as evidence about interrupted financial help, causing an incorrect “COULD NOT FIND.” **Proposed remedy:** absence and evidence searches must operate across raw passages, not only the passage’s current code assignment. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C2-memo.md §One checkable failure]

6. **Synthesis euphemism and moral flattening.** A brother’s unresolved death became “participation delayed or prevented” or a clean determinant of farm succession. **Fix still open:** the blind verdict says this is minted at `SYNTHESIZE`; more doors and a self-brief did not correct it. [engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm3.md §Part 1; engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md §Flattening both share]

7. **Repair-language laundering.** “An advocate changes the travelers’ placement” erased the prior denial of a paid first-class fare and the actor responsible; “repair” made institutional mistreatment sound like a service episode. **Proposed remedy:** preserve the precipitating wrong, entitlement, responsible institution, and degree of repair. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C1-memo.md §The laundering of S7.019]

8. **Unsupported causal merge.** Wartime subsistence sales were merged into “preparing departure,” although tickets came from the father and the farm sales were explicitly capped to protect subsistence. **Proposed remedy:** distinguish adjacency from stated causation and mark analytic joins as inference. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C1-memo.md §The merge in finding 3]

9. **Overbroad network labels.** “Kin and co-regional networks” and “migrant intermediaries” bundled relatives, unrelated travelers, employer housing, and one-off help. **Proposed remedy:** name the observed relation precisely—relative, fellow traveler, company-assigned neighbor, returned migrant—and do not promote two incidents into a stable network without support. [engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm0.md §Part 1; engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm2.md §Part 1; engine/sim_runs/round2/memo-arm3.md §Part 1]

10. **Absence inferred from unread material.** Three earlier simulated researchers rejected a kin claim because no relative appeared in the quarter they had opened, although unread material contained an aunt, uncle, and another aunt. **Fix implemented:** search only the unread material before accepting a negative claim. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §Check an absence]

11. **Unmarked silence.** Explicit `DECLINED` blocks made some exclusions auditable, but topics omitted from both findings and declines remained invisible; immigration inspection was noticed only when the closing battery asked about it. **Proposed remedy:** combine specific declines with section-level analysis-reach coverage. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C1-memo.md §Unmarked silence; engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see]

12. **Claim–evidence misalignment.** Three Round 3 “weakest evidence” doors concerned other topics entirely. **Proposed remedy:** validate that each cited passage semantically supports the exact claim and treat evidence-strength labels as untrusted until that check passes. [engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 1]

13. **Fragmentary free quotations.** A claim can be correct while its free quotation fails to show why. **Proposed remedy:** select a claim-bearing quote or display multiple fragments when the claim depends on separate acts. [engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 1]

14. **Entity and itinerary reconciliation gaps.** The `WHO IS WHO` surface did not reconcile “oldest brother” with the later “half-brother”; partial exposure also made consecutive Aspen and Greeley stops look contradictory. **Proposed remedy:** maintain entity aliases and chronology separately from thematic synthesis, while preserving genuine source uncertainty. [engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t1.md §Who is who; engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 1; engine/sim_runs/round1/C0-manual-memo.md §Battery answers, Q3]

15. **Coverage without discipline.** The self-brief increased coverage but transferred other passengers’ possible exclusion or return onto this family. **Proposed remedy:** keep subject attribution and uncertainty checks independent of breadth incentives. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md §Where A is better]

# 4. DESIGN DECISIONS AND THEIR REASONS

1. **Use simulated delegated reading rather than unrestricted transcript access.** The researcher is forbidden to read or search the transcript because the exercise tests whether a person can supervise delegated analysis under realistic time scarcity. The rejected alternative is full rereading, which would make delegation unnecessary. [engine/sim_runs/round2/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see]

2. **Keep document front matter always visible and free.** Identification and a synopsis are available without spending evidence budget, separating basic document orientation from claim verification. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm0-t1.md §The document]

3. **Budget evidence access.** Round 2 allowed 18 doors over two rounds; Round 3 allowed 30 over three. The reason stated is that researchers delegate because they cannot reread everything. Unlimited access was rejected as inconsistent with the intended use case. [engine/sim_runs/round2/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see; engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see]

4. **Change a door from a sentence ID to a whole exchange.** The exchange preserves question context and prevents several IDs in one answer from consuming several units. Duplicate requests are refunded and named. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see]

5. **Give every claim a free verbatim quote.** Doors should be spent on surrounding context or suspicious support, not merely to discover the basis of every claim. The rejected alternative charged scarce budget for basic claim inspection. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §Claims come with their own words]

6. **Show both analysis reach and researcher reading.** The coverage strip reports, by section, how much the analysis cites and how much the researcher personally opened. This counters the tendency to mistake system coverage—or an unread region—for one’s own knowledge. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see; engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t1.md §The shape of this interview]

7. **Permit doors outside system-proposed citations.** Researchers may use any valid sentence ID, allowing sampling against the analytic frame rather than only within it. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm2-t1.md §The shape of this interview; engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t1.md §The shape of this interview]

8. **Add an explicit absence-check operation.** Negative claims are searched against unread material and returned as supported or refuted with quotes. The rejected alternative was inferring absence from partial exposure. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §Check an absence]

9. **Make stopping early legitimate.** Door expenditure is not itself a goal; researchers may stop when they have enough. This resists a design that confuses budget exhaustion with analytic quality. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §Stop early]

10. **Keep declined and “doesn’t fit yet” material visible.** Excluded material receives a reason, citations, and sometimes a proposed alternative frame, so scope decisions remain contestable. Silent omission was rejected. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm1-t2.md §Doesn’t fit yet; engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm3-t1.md §What this interview changes]

11. **Require explicit epistemic accounting in researcher replies.** Round 2 required `BELIEFS / UNKNOWNS / JUDGMENT / NEXT`; Round 3 added `COVERAGE`. The purpose is to separate knowledge, gaps, evaluation, planned sampling, and the limits imposed by unread material. [engine/sim_runs/round2/reader-brief.md §What to send back; engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §What to send back]

12. **Prefer an honest blank to plausible reconstruction.** Closing instructions require “I don’t know,” forbid period-based reconstruction, and treat confident error as worse than partial knowledge. [engine/sim_runs/round2/final-turn.md §Part 2; engine/sim_runs/round3/final-turn.md §Part 2]

13. **Evaluate substance, texture, inference, and limits separately.** The twelve-question battery prevents an instrument that retrieves events while missing narrative manner, overstates inference, or conceals source limits from appearing successful. [engine/sim_runs/round2/battery-questions.md]

14. **Use a sealed 0–2 rubric.** Each question defines a full answer, partial answer, and failure, including explicit penalties for common inventions. The record does not show completed aggregate scoring. [engine/sim_runs/round2/battery-sealed-for-graders.md §§Q01–Q12]

15. **Test internal prompting with a controlled blind comparison.** Analyses A and B used the same system, starting state, research focus, and number of calls; only one undisclosed mechanism differed. The verdict identifies it as an 89-word self-written brief. This isolates the brief more cleanly than comparing unrelated runs. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4.md §Two analyses of the same interview; engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md]

16. **Use a full manual reading as oracle.** The manual memo states its political-economy standpoint and deliberately tests findings against that instinct, creating a benchmark that includes both interpretation and reflexive limits. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C0-manual-memo.md §Notes on my own reading]

# 5. OPEN QUESTIONS

- No aggregate battery scores are recorded for any arm, so the relative effects of coverage maps, residue displays, self-briefing, free quotes, or absence checks on substantive accuracy remain unquantified. [engine/sim_runs/round2/battery-sealed-for-graders.md; engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md]
- The arm names do not fully disclose every manipulated mechanism, and the blind comparison intentionally withholds the mechanism from its evaluator. A future record needs an explicit arm ledger linking each run to exactly one treatment. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4.md §Two analyses of the same interview]
- The self-written brief was tested on one transcript, one focus, and one blind comparison. Whether its gains and medical-attribution cost generalize is unknown. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md]
- The optimum door budget and exchange size are unresolved. Round 3 improved cumulative exposure to 28.8% but still left entire early sections unread. [engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 1]
- It is unresolved whether exposure should be measured in passages, characters, exchanges, source sentences, or some combination; the files report several units without defining a stable denominator. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm1-t3.md §Turn 3; engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t4.md §The passages you opened]
- The system still needs a synthesis-stage safeguard against euphemism, causal smoothing, and category language that deletes institutional wrongdoing or moral ambiguity. Retrieval improvements alone did not solve this. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md §Flattening both share]
- No reliability evidence is provided for absence checking: its recall, semantic matching behavior, handling of paraphrase, and false-negative rate remain unknown. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §Check an absence]
- Quote selection remains unresolved: how should the system choose the shortest quotation that still carries the full claim, especially when a claim spans multiple acts or exchanges? [engine/sim_runs/round3/memo-r3.md §Part 1]

# 6. VOCABULARY

- **Absence check** — A search of material the researcher has not read, used to support or refute a proposed negative claim with counterevidence if found. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §Check an absence]
- **Anchor** — The person, motive, employment, or relation treated as the organizing center of a movement or settlement claim; it is analytic language, not a UI object. [engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t1.md §§What this interview changes, points 1 and 7]
- **Brief** — Either the reader-facing protocol or, in the blind experiment, an 89-word self-written internal instruction that directs the next analytic pass toward likely omissions and tensions. [engine/sim_runs/round2/screen-arm4-verdict.md]
- **Code** — A lower-level analytic classification supporting a project finding; code counts are bookkeeping, not independent confirmations. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm1-t2.md §Findings]
- **Coverage strip** — Section-by-section display of how much the analysis reaches and how much the researcher personally opened. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see]
- **Declined** — Material explicitly considered but excluded from current findings because its connection to the research focus was judged too indirect. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm0-t1.md §What this interview changes]
- **Door** — In Rounds 1–2, a sentence ID that opens its containing passage; in Round 3, one whole question-and-answer exchange. [engine/sim_runs/round2/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see; engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see]
- **Doesn’t fit yet** — Visible residue that the current codes or focus do not incorporate, retained with evidence and often an offered alternative frame. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm1-t2.md §Doesn’t fit yet]
- **Exchange** — In the Round 3 access model, one interviewer question and its complete answer, treated as the atomic door and billing unit; elsewhere the word also refers ordinarily to barter. [engine/sim_runs/round3/reader-brief.md §What you can and cannot see]
- **Exposure** — The amount of primary material revealed through opened doors, reported as passages, characters, and percentage of the document. [engine/sim_runs/round3/prompt-r3-t4.md §The passages you opened]
- **Finding** — A project-level analytic proposition assembled from one or more codes and labeled with standing, support, evidence, and an ID such as `T01`. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm0-t2.md §Findings]
- **Ledger** — Used metaphorically for an economic/accounting reading that can miss bodily labor; these files do not define a product component named “ledger.” [engine/sim_runs/round1/C1-memo.md §What this interview shows, point 4]
- **Register** — A characteristic vocabulary or manner of telling—economic, apologetic, hedged, minimizing, or emotionally flat—whose source may be the narrator or interviewer. [engine/sim_runs/round1/C0-manual-memo.md §Tensions; engine/sim_runs/round1/C2-memo.md §Notes on working this way]

- **Step** — One surfaced analytic item labeled `PATTERN`, `TENSION`, `UNCERTAINTY`, `DELTA`, or `DECLINED`, with a step ID and supporting doors. [engine/sim_runs/round2/prompt-arm3-t1.md §What this interview changes]
