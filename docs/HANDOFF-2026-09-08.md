## 1. Where the work came from

Five voice messages from a researcher using the live instance on three of her own projects, while
reading interviews she knows well. Four of the five were the same question asked in different
places: *can I tell where this came from, and does the evidence carry it?* Everything in §2 and §3
answers one of them. Nothing here was invented from a code smell.

Her fifth message was praise, and it constrains future work: the line saying how many passages a
theme shares with other themes (`context._evidence`, `context.py:324`) is what she said helps her build a
conceptualisation. Do not remove it. `docs/plans/2026-09-06-fewer-calls.md` finding F1 proposes
changing how overlapping passages are handled, and now has that constraint on it.

## 2. The interviewer is read and never quoted

**The defect.** Claims were resting on verbatim quotes of the person asking the questions. Measured
on the Ellis project in `data/aperture.db`:

| Whose words a live claim rested on | Claims |
|---|---|
| Participant | 126 |
| Interviewer | 12 |

One of the twelve quoted *"Because those borders changed a lot"* — the interviewer's own summary,
presented as a finding about the material.

**Why the anchor law did not catch it.** It cannot. The quote is verbatim, the passage id is right,
and it binds. It is simply the wrong person's verbatim. The researcher's framing settles the design:
it happened where she had summarised the participant *correctly*, so accuracy is not the test and
the rule is absolute rather than a judgement about paraphrase quality.

**Why a prompt rule alone would not have worked.** Eight of the twelve quoted a line carrying no
speaker cue. `layout()` wrote the cue once at the head of a turn and never repeated it, so the model
could not see whose words it was taking. A rule the model cannot see is one it cannot follow, and
PLAN.md §3 law 3 says a slot with no real-output check is a slot you do not have.

**So it is three parts, in the order the anchor law already uses:**

| Part | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| the mechanical fact | `store.asked_sids` | the passages an interviewer spoke, from `sentence.speaker` joined to `speaker.role`. No model involved, same as `turns.scan`. |
| make it visible | `synth.layout` | marks **every** interviewer line, not only turn heads |
| ask, then enforce | `thread.md` rule 11, `synth._thread_kept` | the prompt asks; Python drops a moment bound to an interviewer line and says so in its own words |

**The safety gate that matters.** `asked_sids` returns empty when `material.speakers_estimated` is
set. Where DIARIZE or the transcriber guessed who was speaking, a wrong guess would delete a
participant's words in silence, so there the reading is left alone. This is also where this work
meets the audio path merged from `main`: a transcribed recording numbers the voices the transcriber
separated, so **transcribed recordings get no interviewer protection until someone corrects the
frame.** That is deliberate and it is the safe direction, but it is a real gap to know about.

**What was deliberately not changed.** RESIDUAL drops interviewer passages from its remainder
(`residual.py`) — an unmarked question is not something the coding missed. But READ may still *code*
an interviewer's line, and that is left alone: a question tells you what the topic is, and pointing
the reading at a region is not the same as resting a claim on it. MEMO writes over coded passages
and could therefore still cite one; it is explore-only and opt-in, and it is the one remaining place
prose can rest on an interviewer. Left as a known edge, not fixed.

## 3. Provenance and overclaim

**A citation now says which material it is from.** `context.cite` rendered a bare passage id, and the
corpus summary cites across every material at once. She was reading id numbers for proximity to
guess whether two citations shared a transcript. `_cite_index` had carried `material_id` all along to
build the link with. The page prints the name beside the id; the record writes `[M. Grande S062]`
via `_cite_label`.

**The source-text pane names the text and the theme.** `.record-pane` is `position: sticky` and the
page head is not, so a reader who has scrolled a line of claims has had the material's name off
screen for some time and was being shown an unlabelled window of transcript. It said "Source text"
and "this theme"; it now names both.

**The highlight is visible.** `mark` was `--accent-soft`, eleven per cent of the accent, carrying its
whole meaning in hue. It is twenty per cent with an inset underline, so a highlighted passage is
still one for a reader who does not separate this blue from this grey. The legend in the pane head
carries the same swatch.

**The corpus summary stopped generalising past its evidence.** *"The outputs are never accepted at
face value"*, over a corpus where one participant accepted most of an output and estimated its error
himself. PROJECT rule 5 named "all", "every" and "consistently" and never named the negative, which
is the harder half to see. It does now, and it locks to rule 6: a universal negative not cited
everywhere is an inference from silence, which rule 6 already forbids. `prose.py` gained a
`universal` counter beside it — a counter, never a gate, per the existing pattern.

**The excluded list stopped contradicting itself.** It printed internal moment ids (`mo7aa0189e1`) at
a reader who can neither open nor act on them, and one note reported claims *kept* under a heading
saying *excluded*. Both now say what actually happened.

## 4. The efficiency plan implements nothing

`docs/plans/2026-09-06-fewer-calls.md` is an adversarial read of every `chat_json` call site against
the recorded `call` rows, written as ten findings in a phased order. **No code in it has been
written.** Three measurements carry it:

- THREAD is 44% of output tokens because it is the only step scaling as themes × materials.
- 82% of output is reasoning, so on the default provider the *number* of calls is the bill more than
  the size of any one call.
- The `EFFORT` table in `llm.py` is **inert on MiniMax**, which takes no effort parameter. The
  comment claiming THREAD runs at medium describes Mistral alone. Worth knowing before anyone tunes
  it.

It refuses as much as it proposes: one THREAD call for every theme was measured thin, and the
code-hit gate was measured and rejected in `docs/EVAL.md` pass 3. Phase 1 is the only part that
changes nothing a model sees.

## 5. Deploy — open, and needs a human

`main` has the code. The server does not. Its stylesheet still reports
`last-modified: Mon, 07 Sep 2026 15:45:34 GMT`, byte-identical to `60b9153`.

**Root cause:** Coolify is configured for GitHub source deploys and holds a webhook secret, but
`RJuro/aperture` **has no webhook at all** (`gh api repos/RJuro/aperture/hooks` returns `[]`).
Nothing tells Coolify a push happened. Yesterday's deploys were almost certainly triggered by hand.
`docs/DEPLOY.md` says every push to `main` redeploys; **that is not currently true.**

**The correct application.** Do not reuse `COOLIFY_MASSHINE_APP_UUID` from the MASSHINE engine's
`.env` — that is the `masshine` app, the predecessor, the one that already lost a corpus once.

| | |
|---|---|
| name | `aperture` |
| uuid | `kzk8goddckev3r6eqwl9gc5n` |
| repo | `RJuro/aperture`, branch `main` |

Deploy, sourcing your own env file so no token is pasted anywhere:

```bash
set -a; . "$MASSHINE_ENGINE/.env"; set +a; curl -s "$COOLIFY_URL/api/v1/deploy?uuid=kzk8goddckev3r6eqwl9gc5n&force=true" -H "Authorization: Bearer $COOLIFY_TOKEN"
```

Then this returns `2` when the new build is serving, and needs no sign-in:

```bash
curl -s https://aperture.automate.business.aau.dk/static/aperture.css | grep -c cite-who
```

**Safe to deploy.** Storage is attached at `/data` (Coolify storage 25, `docs/WORKPLAN.md`), so a
redeploy does not wipe the database, and `resume_pending` relaunches an interrupted analysis. A
researcher may be mid-session; her running job will resume, not vanish.

**Two follow-ups:** add the missing GitHub webhook so `main` actually auto-deploys, and rotate the
MiniMax key, Mistral key and Coolify token in the MASSHINE engine `.env` — they were read into an
assistant transcript during this session.

## 8. What not to undo

- `asked_sids` returning empty on an estimated frame. It looks like a missing feature; it is the
  guard that stops a transcriber's guess deleting a participant's words.
- The dropped-moment note saying the quote is the interviewer's rather than that it is missing. The
  quote *is* in the material, and the other wording sends a researcher hunting a fault that is not
  there.
- The shared-passages line on the theme reach (§1).
- `prose.py` counting rather than rejecting. Every counter in that file is read beside a reader's
  judgement, never in place of one — `docs/EVAL.md`'s rule, and the reason a retry is not spent on
  style.
