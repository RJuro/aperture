Introduction

How reading works here

Aperture is not a chat window, and there is no prompt to write. You give it your own material; it reads, and everything it writes stays tied to the words it read. Five things worth knowing before you start.

{% for label in steps %} {% set n = loop.index %} {{ n }}{{ label }} {% endfor %}

{{ at }} of {{ steps | length }}

{% if at == 1 %}

You bring the material. Nothing else is read.

Interviews, field notes, documents, open responses, recordings. Everything Aperture writes is written about those files and about nothing else — it brings no general knowledge to them, and there is no conversation to have with it. Add a material and the reading begins on its own.

It takes minutes rather than seconds, and it continues if you close the page.

In a project: Add material

{% elif at == 2 %}

Nothing is claimed without the words it came from.

Every claim carries a short quotation from your material, and the quotation is a link: select it and you land on that sentence, marked, in the transcript itself. You are never asked to take a finding on trust.

A claim whose quotation cannot be found in the material is not kept. It is set aside, and the page prints it under Excluded from the analysis with the reason — so a reading that overreached is visible rather than quietly tidied away.

On a material: the quotation under each claim

{% elif at == 3 %}

Themes are counted, and it is you who promotes them.

A pattern found in one material is a candidate, and stays one. It becomes a theme of the project when enough of your cases carry it — and even then the instrument only counts and puts the question; adding it to the project is your decision, not its.

Lock a theme's definition and later material is read against those words instead of quietly rewording them. What pulls against a locked definition is kept beside it as a note, which is the case for unlocking it again.

On the project: the table of themes by material

{% elif at == 4 %}

An empty cell is a question, not a verdict.

Where a theme has nothing in a material, the table says which kind of nothing it is: never looked for, looked for and too thin to keep, passed over by a preliminary review, or not assessed yet. Four different findings that would otherwise all be blank.

Absence is something you run. Check a theme against a material and read what comes back — the answer says what was searched, so "not found" means something you can report.

On the project: the empty cells, and Check a claim

{% else %}

You answer the reading. It does not answer you.

A comment goes onto one piece of the analysis — a line of claims, a theme, a summary — and is used the next time that piece is written. Nothing replies to you in a chat window; the writing changes instead, and the old version stays in the record.

Every step, every model call and what each one cost is printed in the analysis record. You can always say how a sentence in your findings came to be there.
