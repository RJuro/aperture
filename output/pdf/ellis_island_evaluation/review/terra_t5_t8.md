# Terra evidence review: T5–T8

## Scope and method

I close-read the complete source transcripts for Elizabeth Friedman (T5), Mary Masare Thome (T6), Yolan Szency Batta (T7), and Charles Rizzuto (T8), then reviewed all 157 corresponding `segment_alignment.json` rows (111–267), the original human rows/annotations/memos, the adjudicated code sets, and the machine claim inventory. The companion JSON gives one manual relation per consolidated row. This is a semantic evidence review, not a label-string or similarity exercise: a human code counts when its defensible substantive meaning is supported, including when only one coder expressed it cleanly.

`substantive_match` means at least one cited machine claim anywhere in the same transcript carries the grounded human meaning. `partial_or_different_lens` always cites the narrower or differently focused AI claim. `human_insight_missing` means no substantively equivalent claim was found anywhere in that transcript, not merely that the crosswalk failed to attach one. `unsupported_human_inference` identifies a code whose proposed meaning is not supported by its source fragment; it is analytically separate from whether AI has an accurate claim about some other aspect of that fragment. The JSON separately records qualified/unsupported human components where needed.

The alignment’s `machine_covered: false` flag is not treated as a qualitative verdict. Two false negatives are material: row 159 (children’s better conditions) and row 160 (hardship ‘worth it’) are substantively covered by nearby T5 AI claims even though the crosswalk did not attach them. Row 261 likewise expresses T8 reunification already represented in AI’s migration-arrangement claim.

## Strongest evidence cases

1. **T5, row 114 — passage money displaced by survival.** Friedman says the family paid for a private cabin because the crossing was “terrible,” using money intended for life after arrival. The human readings of difficult travel and drained resources are both warranted. The AI’s `economic-loss-defeating-migration-s-educational-promise:1` retains the important tradeoff (source passage S104), rather than reducing it to generic seasickness.

2. **T5, rows 134–43 — security, violence, and social protection are distinct.** Being Jewish is described as insecure despite comfort, followed by the Beilis period, pogrom care for an injured woman, and Maria’s icon-based intervention. AI claims `violence-as-habitual-backdrop:2`, `:7`, and `:11` support the human meanings. This is better evidence for non-economic migration pressure than an undifferentiated “hardship” code.

3. **T5, rows 144–45 — distinguish a broad AI match from a real omission.** Friedman says Christmas ornaments made her nauseated for years because of the ship’s rocking, and separately says the boat had “no niceties.” AI’s S441 claim about hardship leaving emotional residue covers the first meaning at a broad level, but no T5 claim captures the second travel-condition account. This is why the review does not equate missing crosswalk links with missing substantive coverage.

4. **T5, rows 154–65 — language has both cost and later civic value.** Rapid English enables grade skipping but permanently leaves a mathematics gap; Russian mostly disappears at home yet retained Russian later enables translating for newcomers and the Red Cross. The AI strongly covers these links (`language-as-barrier-and-gateway:7`; `absence-of-arrival-support-prompting-later-volunteerism:5`). Friedman says that bereavement left her lost, then volunteering increased and “did a great deal” for her; thus C2’s restorative/healing reading has contextual support. The AI’s *mirroring her own arrival* causal gloss is not supported; the explicit trigger is bereavement.

5. **T6, rows 168, 173, 180, 185, 207–08 — women’s and children’s labor is dense and concrete.** Bread for twenty, washboard labor, pre-school chores, boarding-house work paid in family food, and few happy childhood memories give the human labor/hardship coding unusually strong evidentiary depth. AI covers several individual tasks, but misses the explicit retrospective impact in row 208. The close reading favors retaining both task-level and life-course meanings.

6. **T6, rows 176 and 193 — small material/ethical details test claim granularity.** AI S248 records the mother carrying trunk, bundle, and both children, but omits the human’s “all worldly goods” material-precarity specificity. Fetching water for passengers unable to obtain it has no equivalent T6 claim and is a genuine mutual-care omission. C3’s “receiving help from others” at row 176 is not supported by that passage.

7. **T6, rows 189 and 210–13 — institutional language and medical fear.** Punishment for Slovak outside the home, concealing Michael’s boil, and the prospect of being returned if illness were serious link language discipline and health inspection to state power. AI captures these well (`language-as-barrier-and-gateway:3`; `ellis-island-as-a-child-s-threshold:2`, `:8`).

8. **T7, rows 220 and 227 — provenance errors distort otherwise useful AI coverage.** Batta explicitly identifies as “Catholic. Roman Catholic,” reports Catholic school and Mass, and only says that she had Jewish friends in Hungary and used the Jewish kitchen at Ellis Island. Thus a Jewish *identity* or “cultural continuity” interpretation is unsupported. The defensible code is a narrower one: familiar social ties/food navigation during detention. AI correctly records the thirty-two co-travellers (S347), but its recurring “child/childhood threshold” framing is also a provenance error: T7’s header gives age 24 at 1921 arrival.

9. **T7, rows 219 and 228–38 — detention is neither simply hardship nor simple support.** Her aunt’s political connection leads to a presidential telegram and release, while her father feels like a stranger after long separation. At the same time, the thirty-two co-travellers and the “monkey cage” girlfriend offer peer contact inside a highly contingent detention setting. The AI covers the procedural chain and peer support but misses the initial group-migration fact (row 227).

10. **T8, rows 245–56 — AI’s community and continuity framing needs restraint.** Rizzuto directly says the neighborhood was Italian/Sicilian and full of relations, so ethnic-network settlement is supported. But a short fragment cannot establish a broad, durable “cultural continuity” thesis by itself. Likewise, a single wool mattress is an item brought from home, not evidence of an encompassing continuity structure. The source clearly identifies Charles as male; any session language treating this speaker as “she” is a provenance error.

11. **T8, rows 252 and 254 — gendered household arrangements are unusually explicit.** Grandfather gives houses to married boys while telling girls to rely on husbands; Rizzuto’s mother calls him “the man of the house” and leaves a church contribution to him. The AI captures both, though phrases such as “religious practice conferred authority” add theoretical framing beyond the testimony. The human meanings remain well supported.

12. **T8, rows 257, 259, and 264 — the missed ordinary details are analytically useful.** Smuggling wine, a bullying-linked compound arm fracture, and miserable housing with a hall toilet and no running water are explicit and material. They are not merely color: together they show everyday constraint after arrival that the claim inventory’s larger themes underrepresent.

## Provenance and row-count checks

The C2 source sheet declares **159 rows**, but has **158 non-empty initial-code cells**. The extra T1 row has an annotation about adults concealing information from a child but no `initial_codes` value; it is the likely explanation for 159 source rows versus 158 occupied consolidated C2 cells. In the T5–T8 subset, C2 has 82 non-empty original code rows and the crosswalk has 82 occupied C2 cells. I found no T5–T8 merge/drop discrepancy.

Machine claims are often usefully specific, especially where quotation is close. The recurrent limitation is added causal or thematic framing: “mirroring” one’s arrival, treating a brief object as social continuity, or claiming a broader structural effect than the quoted turn establishes. Conversely, missing mundane but concrete testimony (food, water-carrying, housing, injury, sensory memory) is a real coverage weakness rather than evidence that those human codes are invalid.
